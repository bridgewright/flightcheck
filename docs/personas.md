# Who flightcheck is for — customer personas & needs

The reference document for every product and design decision. If a screen,
a sentence of copy, or a color choice can't be defended in front of one of
these people, it doesn't ship. (Personas are market archetypes drawn from
the target segment — non-native English speakers interviewing at global
tech companies — not any real individual.)

---

## The shared situation

Our customers are **good at their jobs and unproven in English interviews.**
They have real accomplishments in their home market and a live ambition to
work on a global stage — and between those two facts sits a 45-minute
English conversation that currently defeats them. Not for lack of substance:
for lack of reps. There is no safe place to fail out loud, in English, at
the actual bar of the actual role, and then be told the truth about it.

They practice at night, after work, alone. They are disciplined,
achievement-driven, and quietly anxious about one specific thing: *sounding
smaller than they are.*

## P1 — The proven operator aiming abroad (primary)

- **Who**: Korean senior IC / consultant / PM, 4–10 years in. Strong
  domestic track record. Reads and writes English professionally; freezes
  when interviewing in it live.
- **Target**: FDPM / AI-deployment / product roles at global tech companies
  (US, Singapore, EU). Often preparing quietly while employed.
- **Pain**: mock interviews with friends are socially padded ("you did
  great"); coaches are expensive, scheduled, and generic; practicing alone
  produces fluency in monologue, not in being interrupted, probed, and
  time-boxed.
- **Needs**: ① the *real* bar of a specific JD, not generic questions
  ② shame-free repetition — an interviewer with no memory of last night's
  bad answer ③ honest verdicts with specifics ("lead with the decision,
  then the story") ④ delivery judged from actual voice, not a transcript
  ⑤ visible progress — fuel to keep going at 11 PM on a Tuesday.
- **Buying trigger**: sees one honest, specific report on their own answer.

## P2 — The non-Korean global aspirant (secondary)

- **Who**: non-native speaker elsewhere (Japan, Vietnam, LATAM, Europe)
  with the same shape of ambition and the same live-English gap; different
  L1 interference patterns.
- **Why they matter now**: they prove the product's rules are about
  *non-native interviewing*, not Korean-specific coaching — the premise of
  global expansion. English-only UI is deliberate for them.
- **Distinct need**: never being treated as an edge case — the product must
  feel native to them, not localized *from* someone else.

## P3 — The deadline switcher (urgent variant of P1/P2)

- **Who**: has a real interview scheduled in 2–3 weeks. Found us while
  cram-searching.
- **Distinct needs**: zero-friction start (JD in, interviewing within
  minutes), intensity (multiple sessions/week against ONE JD), and a
  brutally clear "what to fix first" ranking. Patience for onboarding: none.
- **Buying trigger**: the demo video showing a real session — if the
  interviewer sounds real, the card comes out.

## What all three reward

| They value | So the product must | Design translation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Truth over comfort | Honest verdicts, no grade inflation | Confident, unapologetic copy; no confetti |
| Their time (nights, fragments) | 20-min sessions, instant resume, zero ceremony | Calm surfaces, one primary action per screen |
| Feeling like a professional, not a student | Coach-peer tone, never gamified kiddie UI | Premium, precise, adult aesthetics |
| Evidence of climb | Trends, per-dimension movement, "ready" trajectory | Progress as instrumentation, not badges |
| The ambition itself | Honor the leap they're attempting | The journey/flight identity: preparation as preflight |

## Anti-personas (not designing for)

- Native speakers polishing negotiation tactics.
- Students seeking generic behavioral-question banks.
- Anyone wanting flattery. The product's promise is the bar, not a boost.
