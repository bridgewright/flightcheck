# flightcheck v0.8.0

Released: 2026-08-04

One door in, and two ways out.

Sign-in stopped being a message that has to arrive and became a Google button.
A scored report stopped being a page behind an account and became a file the
customer holds. And a session whose tab died stopped costing its owner
twenty-four minutes of waiting.

## What shipped

**Sign-in is Google, and it is the only door** (DECISIONS 036, which supersedes
the passwordless half of 022). `signInWithOtp`, the email field, the sent state
and its resend cooldown are gone, and so is the flag that used to hide the
Google button while the provider was unconfigured. The email provider is
disabled in the hosted project too, so the removal holds at the API rather than
only in the UI. `/settings` lost its change-sign-in-address form with it: the
address belongs to the Google account now, and a form there would have changed
a row without changing a credential.

The reason is the failure mode the old door had. Passwordless made email
delivery the entire auth surface, on a sender the product does not control and
whose own documentation calls it rate-limited and best-effort, under a message
that never said "flightcheck", carried "powered by Supabase", and offered to
unsubscribe the reader from their own sign-in link. Every account it created was
one undelivered message away from being unreachable, including accounts holding
a paid package.

The cost is stated rather than hedged: **a customer with no Google account
cannot sign in at all.** There is no fallback door by design.

Existing accounts were not stranded, and that was verified by signing in rather
than by reading a settings page back: Supabase links a Google identity to a user
whose email is confirmed, and the production sign-in landed on the existing
account with its package, its session history and its paid order intact (users
2 before and after, identities 2 to 3, one google and one email identity on the
same `user_id`).

**A scored report leaves the product as a file** (F-49). Two controls on a
session: Download PDF and Download Markdown. The PDF is a real file arriving in
Downloads rather than a print dialog, rendered server-side without a headless
browser on the request path, on white rather than the product's warm paper
ground, because a report that is printed or attached is read on white. Both
formats carry the same facts in the same order, the limits note verbatim, and
every evidence quote unparaphrased.

Nothing is stored. Each file is generated when it is asked for and streamed, so
the export adds no new place a customer's data lives and nothing extra to
delete; the privacy page says so, including the part that is not comfortable,
which is that a downloaded file is past our reach. The export is exactly as
authorized as the page it comes from, and a capability link's export dies with
the link.

**A session that stopped reporting can be reclaimed by its owner** (F-47). The
room beats every fifteen seconds; a session silent for sixty seconds may be
reclaimed from `/home` by the account that owns it, and the slot is not
consumed. Before this, the only thing that released the one-live-session lock
(DECISIONS 028) was the twenty-five-minute hard cut, so a customer whose tab
crashed one minute in had no route back. A session with no heartbeat on record
behaves exactly as it did before. Migration `007_session_heartbeat.sql`, applied
before the worker that reads the column, in that order.

## What the review caught, and what it says about the process

Both feature tracks were implemented by delegated agents against written briefs
and reviewed at merge. Three findings are worth publishing.

**The export would have answered 404 for every report already in the database.**
It gated on `report.eligibility === "scored"`, which is correct against every
fixture in the test suite and wrong against production: reports stored before
F-03 and F-04 carry ten keys, and neither `eligibility` nor `headline` is among
them. No test with a current fixture could have found it. A query against the
real database did. The export now decides on the row's status, which is what the
screen decides on.

**A gate can be satisfied without being met.** The PDF module needs literal
colour, because a PDF has no stylesheet and cannot read a design token. The
first version assembled its hexes from a `#` constant, which passes the
raw-colour scan while declaring nothing. This suite already had the honest
mechanism for that case: a named exemption carrying the reason. The file takes
it, and a test pins its five values so the export cannot quietly grow a sixth or
reach for the paper ground.

**An instruction about new text became licence to edit old text.** A brief said
no em-dashes in files the track touches; the track rewrote eight of them in
prose it was only passing through. Restored at merge.
