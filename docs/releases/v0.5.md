# flightcheck v0.5.0

Released: 2026-08-03

## Why the tags jump from v0.2 to v0.5

The published plan (DECISIONS #008, and the README that carried it) read
v0.3 report quality → v0.4 session history → v0.5 curriculum-lite → then
payments. What actually happened: the 0.3 and 0.4 work shipped, inside the
parallel batches of 2026-08-02 and 2026-08-03, and **was never tagged**.
Payments then shipped *as* v0.5 rather than after it, and curriculum-lite
moved to v0.6+.

So there is no `v0.3.0` and no `v0.4.0`, and neither is being created after
the fact — a tag invented retroactively is a decoration, not a release rhythm.
(Refined on 2026-08-04, when `v0.6.0` and `v0.7.0` *were* cut after the fact:
the rule is that a tag may be cut afterwards **at the commit that was actually
deployed**, and may not be invented for a boundary that never shipped
separately. 0.3 and 0.4 are the second kind and are still not tagged.
DECISIONS 032.)
The tag line of record **through this release** is v0.1.0 → v0.2.0 → v0.5.0,
and this document covers
everything between the second and the third. The version numbers were left
where the plan put them rather than renumbered down to v0.3, because the plan
is on the record in DECISIONS #008 and quietly renumbering it would hide the
miss instead of logging it. The correction is written into that entry.

## What shipped

- **The product can take money.** Checkout runs through Polar as merchant of
  record — the app never renders a card field — and a signature-verified,
  replay-safe `order.paid` webhook is the only thing that unlocks a package.
  A forged or unsigned webhook provisions nothing; a missing webhook secret
  refuses to process at all. **No sale has happened** — see Known limits
  before reading anything else into this.
- **Trial, then unlock.** A package's first session is free and fully scored.
  $49 unlocks that same package — same JD, same rubric — to six 20-minute
  sessions, and a 30-day window starts at payment (not at package creation:
  nobody pays for compile time or shelf time). Reports, transcripts, and
  replays stay readable after expiry. One price, one currency, one constant
  (`apps/web/lib/pricing.ts`) with a test pinning it; the old ₩89,000 figure
  is retired everywhere including the legal pages, which render their
  commercial numbers from the same constant so they cannot drift.
- **Accounts, and a real app behind them.** Sign-in is a passwordless email
  link (Supabase Auth) — no password exists anywhere in the product. The
  Google provider is implemented but shipped behind a disabled flag
  (`GOOGLE_AUTH_ENABLED = false` in `apps/web/app/login/page.tsx`) until the
  OAuth provider is configured, so email links are the only live method
  today. Canonical URLs moved to login-scoped ids; the old `/p/[token]`
  links are demoted to claim addresses that bind a package to the first
  signed-in account, redirect its owner, and answer a foreign owner with a
  real 403 page. A capability token in a URL was the wrong risk profile for
  pages that now carry a person's recorded voice (DECISIONS #012).
- **The session archive, with the evidence attached.** Every session is one
  honest row — number, date, status or verdict, score, and movement against
  your previous scored session. Session detail carries the report with
  per-dimension deltas, a verbatim transcript viewer with the delivery
  judge's observations inlined at their timestamps, and audio replay of the
  exact recording that was scored, with timestamps that seek the player.
  This is the proof-of-fairness surface: *this is what was scored, nothing
  else.* Transcripts are persisted immediately after transcription and before
  any judging, so a session whose scoring later fails still keeps its record
  (DECISIONS #013); sessions from before this honestly show "transcript
  unavailable" rather than silently regenerating one.
- **Progress, as instrumentation rather than encouragement.** Verdict and
  overall trajectory across the package, per-dimension trend rows, delivery
  trends (pace, fillers), and a recurring-focus callout computed by fixed
  rules. No badges, no streaks.
- **The bar is visible before you sit down.** A rubric page shows your role's
  dimensions, weights, signals, and score anchors. The question bank is
  deliberately withheld: a candidate who rehearses the literal probes gets a
  verdict about their rehearsal, which is the one thing this product cannot
  sell (DECISIONS #015).
- **Thin sessions are not scored at all.** Below the evidence floors
  (600 s, 5 candidate turns, 200 candidate words) a session ends
  `insufficient` — zero judge calls, no report, an honest "not enough
  evidence" state, and the slot stays available. Between the floors and the
  full-evidence bars the report ships marked `limited` and says so. A real
  interviewer who saw four minutes would say "we did not see enough"; the
  floors are that sentence in code (DECISIONS #014). Reports also open with
  a one-line headline — the verdict and the dimension holding it back.
- **The interviewer is locked to English.** Whatever language the candidate
  answers in, the interviewer replies in English: one warm invitation back
  the first time, no comment after that. The English session is the training,
  so this is a product rule, not a limitation.
- **Guardrails a paid product needs.** Per-user rate limits on package,
  session, and scoring creation; input length caps with base64 size checked
  before decoding; a recording-duration ceiling at scoring intake; retry caps
  that retire a slot honestly instead of looping; a reaper that fails
  packages and sessions stalled mid-pipeline past 30 minutes instead of
  letting them spin forever; worker logs visible in production and an error
  tracker that switches on only when configured.
- **The injection minimum.** Every user-controlled text (JD, resume,
  LinkedIn, transcript) is delimiter-fenced at every prompt site with
  fence-marker neutralization, the JD is truncated before rubric
  compilation, and hidden HTML/zero-width/bidi payloads are stripped once at
  intake. Judge instructions are untouched. Detection-and-refusal and an
  injection eval suite are explicitly *not* in this release (DECISIONS #021)
  — see Known limits.
- **Legal and trust surfaces.** Terms, privacy, and refund pages in the
  product's own plain register, linked from a footer on every app page.
  Deletion today is a prefilled email from settings executed operator-side
  by a dry-run-by-default purge tool, within a stated 7 days — a manual
  process honestly described, rather than a self-serve flow shipped untested
  (DECISIONS #020). The refund rule is stated rather than hedged: the verdict
  itself is never refund grounds; technical failures refund within 14 days.
- **The session room survives Safari and iOS.** Recorder construction moved
  inside the failure path with a container probe and an `audio/mp4` fallback,
  audio starts inside the user's own tap, and a capability gate turns an
  unsupported browser into an honest message *before* the mic check instead
  of a permanent silent "Connecting…". Start-session refusals now name what
  happened — package exhausted, package expired, slot closed, rate limited,
  service unreachable — each with its own calm state. A recording disclosure
  line at the mic check says plainly that the session is recorded, scored,
  and kept privately for replay.
- **The conversation clock is entirely client-owned, and adversarially
  tested.** `create_response: false` means the server never speaks on its
  own; a pure, unit-tested reducer is the only source of `response.create`,
  with a debounced response, sub-2 s filler tolerance, and staged silence
  scaffolds at 8 s / 15 s / 30 s (DECISIONS #009, #011). A seeded adversarial
  suite attacks that reducer on every test run — five generators plus a
  5,000-tick soup, checked against six invariants by an independently written
  shadow model — and found two real clock faults before any user did.

it has not happened yet; that is the third release in a row saying so, and it
is recorded here rather than dropped.

## Known limits

- **The one payment on the books is the operator's own.** The live path was
  verified by the operator's own $49 purchase, provisioned end to end and then
  self-refunded with the receipt kept — a self-test, not a sale, with zero
  external customers and zero revenue. It proves the checkout → webhook →
  provisioning path works end to end in production. It is not demand
  evidence, not revenue, and not a first customer. flightcheck still has zero
  external users, and no usage numbers are claimed anywhere in this repo
  (`docs/metrics/`).
- **Four things this release shipped are not covered by the eval gate**, and
  saying so is the point:
  - *Prompt-injection resistance.* The fencing, truncation, and hidden-text
    strip have unit tests only. Layer 1 scores against a committed rubric and
    never invokes the rubric compiler, so the JD truncation that runs before
    the compiler has no gate coverage at all. The adversarial injection suite
    is F-11b, in v0.6.
  - *The scoring-eligibility gate.* The floors and the `limited` band are
    unit-tested; no eval suite measures whether they land in the right place
    on real sessions. Checking them needs a real duration distribution
    (F-13, v0.6).
  - *Report field quality.* The v0.5 gate measures rubric discrimination and
    the delivery judge, not the text a report renders. The one-line headline
    is synthesized deterministically at compile time, and
    `DimensionScore.strengths`/`weaknesses` still ship as **empty defaults**
    pending a judge-authored pass validated at a gate. DECISIONS #016 set
    that revisit for the next eval-gated release; the gate fired and the
    judge-authored pass did not ride it.
  - *The package and payment lifecycle.* Trial quota, the paid unlock, the
    30-day expiry, and webhook idempotency are covered by unit and route
    tests plus the single live purchase above. No eval suite exercises them.
- **Cohen's κ has no instrument.** The PRD carries judge–human scoring
  agreement ≥ 0.8 as a v1.0 target; no suite computes κ, and none is built.
  The only agreement evidence today is an interim proxy: on the three golden
  triplets the human blind rankings and the judge each match the key 3/3 —
  ordering agreement at N=3, which is not κ and should not be read as κ.
- **The trial is per package, not per account.** The worker's quota
  chokepoint keys on `paid_at` alone, so every unpaid package grants a
  one-session trial, not only an account's first; `is_trial` marks records
  and copy but never quota. A second JD therefore also gets one free session
  before its $49 unlock. Bounded by the 10-package cap and per-user create
  rate limits, recorded openly in DECISIONS #018 rather than discovered
  later.
- **Deletion is manual, and nothing expires on its own.** A prefilled email
  starts it and the operator runs a dry-run-by-default purge tool within a
  stated 7 days. Self-serve deletion is F-34, in v0.6, alongside email
  change. Recordings and transcripts are still retained indefinitely — there
  is no automatic deletion (DECISIONS #013, #020).
- **No per-session cost metering, and no unit economics.** Promised for v0.2,
  still not shipped; it lands with the first real-usage metrics (F-13, v0.6).
- **The demo GIF is a v0.2 capture.** It shows intake, the compiled rubric,
  the session-room clock, and the report — not sign-in, pricing, checkout,
  the session archive, or the progress screen, i.e. not most of what this
  release added. It gets re-recorded when the visual design pass lands.
- **The interviewer's behaviors remain instruction-shaped.** The opening
  beat, pause tolerance, restatement, and the English lock were confirmed by
  feel in real sessions; consistency across sessions is still not measured.
- **Everything in the [v0.1](v0.1.md#known-limits) and
  [v0.2](v0.2.md#known-limits) known limits still applies**, except the two
  this release closed: one baseline session per package (packages now carry
  six), and very short sessions being scored like full ones (the eligibility
  floors handle that now).
