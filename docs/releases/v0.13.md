# flightcheck v0.13.0

Released: 2026-08-10

The sessions stop repeating, the interview learns who is sitting in
the chair, and the front door gets lighter — a five-minute quick
interview replaces the free trial session as the one free door.

Disclosure carried from the changelog, so the numbers stay honest:
flightcheck still has no external users. Every session behind this
release's claims was run by the operator, and one synthetic session
exists in the database, disclosed and excluded from anything that
will ever be called usage.

## What shipped

**Fresh topics, for real this time** (F-83, DECISIONS 058). Each of a
package's six sessions now draws different questions from the
rubric's own bank — no verbatim repeats across the package, enforced
for every string the interviewer speaks: main questions, follow-up
probes, and the pressure moment, which rotates through six distinct
challenges and aims at the weakest scored dimension from the
candidate's own history. From the second session with a scored
report, the sequence leads with what the last report said needs work.
Deterministic from the rubric, the session number, and stored
history: no model call at session start, and a committed golden
fixture pins that session one is byte-identical to what it always
was. Shipping this retired the standing honesty caveat that every
session ran the same plan — the ledger entries record their own
closure rather than disappearing.

**The person, not just the role** (F-80, DECISIONS 059; the fourth
faithfulness fixture). When a resume or LinkedIn profile is on the
package, the rubric carries one profile-licensed "Role & company fit"
dimension — scored from the transcript, reported, coached, and
trended like every other dimension, with an honest receipt on the
rubric screen: this bar comes from your own profile, not a JD quote
it does not have. The interviewer asks one or two personal questions
built from the actual career story — why this company, why this role
after that career — rotating angles across sessions. The JD-evidence
contract is not weakened: only this one dimension may carry the
profile license, refused independently by the compiler and by the
eval layer, and the release gate now proves it live on a fourth
committed fixture (4/4, floor 1.0 —
[gate report](../../evals/reports/2026-08-10-v013-gate.md)).

**A five-minute quick interview is the new front door** (F-84,
DECISIONS 060; migration 012). Type a target company and a role — no
job description — sign in, and talk to the interviewer for five
minutes. The questions are templated from those two fields alone and
say so: no research pass, no rubric, nothing recorded, nothing
scored. The screen after it shows the public sample report framed
with the visitor's own company and role, under a banner that says
exactly what it is: a sample, personal only when a package is bought.
Quick sessions ride the same room and closing auto-end at a 5-minute
budget with an 8-minute hard cut, pinned by the same cross-language
timing gates as the 20-minute pair. Each account holds a small
lifetime allowance of quick interviews — they cost real
realtime-API money per minute.

**The free trial session retires** (DECISIONS 060). The first package
no longer carries a free scored session: the quick interview is the
one free door, registering a job description stays free up to the
compiled rubric — the bar made visible before any payment — and
every full 20-minute scored session is paid. Historical trial rows
keep their data; the flag is never granted again. Every surface that
sold the trial — landing, pricing, FAQ, terms, refund, checkout,
llms.txt — now describes the real model, held by a copy gate that
fails if the retired claims return. The quota chokepoint exposes
zero sessions on a locked package, and the realtime-secret mint
refuses locked and expired packages outright — the door a
pre-existing session row used to hold open. A review round also
proved the quick allowance was a delete button away from unlimited
(deleting a quick package reopened the lifetime cap), so per-package
deletion of quick rows is refused after ownership is proven; account
deletion still removes everything. The 060 amendment records the
trade.

**The home screen introduces itself, once** (DECISIONS 061). The
first-ever arrival on home dims the page and walks the features one
by one — Sessions, Progress, the rubric, the start button —
skippable at every step, Escape ends it, shown once per browser. The
product's only auto-opening surface, on purpose.

## Field notes, honestly

Every track in this release went through two adversarial review
rounds, and in all three the second round broke something the first
round had fixed. The batch's own record keeps the sharpest ones: a
client function posting to a route that did not exist, green the
whole way because fetch was stubbed; the quick-package allowance
resettable from the customer's own delete button; the unscored
room's ending offering an upload retry for a recording that was
never made; a copy gate defeated by a line wrap. All fixed before
this tag, all in [RETRO.md](../../RETRO.md).
