# flightcheck v0.12.0

Released: 2026-08-08

The product now coaches you after the interview, and the interview
itself gets out of your way.

Disclosure carried from the changelog, so the numbers stay honest:
every "customer" in this release's story is the operator running the
product as a customer. flightcheck still has no external users; these
were real sessions, not real usage.

## What shipped

**Every answer gets a coach** (F-73/F-77, DECISIONS 049, 053, 057;
migration 010). After scoring, each candidate answer in the transcript
carries a verdict check — strong, or worth reworking; very short
answers are left alone. Opening a check expands an in-flow card that
leads with the whole answer rewritten stronger, then a short why
naming the specific phrases, then what was actually said, verbatim.
Suggestions are generated text and say so; the transcript and every
quoted anchor stay verbatim under the same contract the report
enforces, and an anchor that cannot be located drops its suggestion
rather than shipping a misquote. Each suggestion can be bookmarked,
and a flag reports what went wrong — misheard speech, inappropriate
content, an inaccurate paraphrase, a missing explanation, or your own
words — stored as structured data, because a reader's distrust is
exactly what a coaching-quality evaluation needs. Coaching is
generated right after scoring, never delays or fails a report, and is
not produced for sessions the eligibility gate refused to score.

**Study notes live inside each session** (F-77, DECISIONS 053;
migration 011). Bookmarking builds the session's wrong-answer
notebook: each saved item becomes an interviewer-question and answer
set — the question, the better answer, what you said, why — joined by
the session's core questions with model answers, the expressions worth
keeping, and the session review. Exportable per session as Markdown or
PDF in plain question-and-answer form. Nothing is built on demand and
nothing goes stale: it all derives from artifacts the scoring pass
already made plus your own bookmarks. (An earlier in-development
shape — a package-level Study tab with a generated guide behind a
button — was superseded before any release carried it; its
`study_materials` table ships dormant.)

**The home tab answers "am I getting better"** (F-72/F-78, DECISIONS
054). Overall score trend, per-dimension trajectories, and recurring
gaps, composed above the session list. Every rubric trend draws as a
line: a tall overall chart with all six package sessions on the axis —
scored ones plotted, future ones visibly waiting — and per-dimension
sparklines, the data line in ink over a translucent pastel wash. The
first shape, fixed-width bars, collapsed into an unreadable smear the
moment the column narrowed; the redesign came from the operator
reading the dashboard as a customer and is recorded, contrast
measurements and all.

**Feedback becomes a form that remembers** (F-71, DECISIONS 048;
migration 009). The footer's Feedback door stops being a mailto: a
five-star rating in half-star steps plus optional text, stored with
rate limits and size caps, and an operator-only inbox to read and
manage what arrives. Feedback text is untrusted: escaped on render,
never fed to a model.

**The interviewer answers about 0.6 seconds sooner** (F-81, DECISIONS
055). The wait after an answer was a 0.9 s speech-detection tail plus
a 1.2 s client debounce held open for hesitant speakers; field use
called the total visibly too slow. The debounce drops to 0.6 s — the
acoustic tail stays, and 1.5 s of required quiet still protects a
thinking pause. This executes the original decision's own revisit
condition.

**The interviewer's goodbye ends the session** (F-81, DECISIONS 056).
The room recognizes the closing line — armed only in the final two
minutes, so mid-session small talk cannot trip it — retires the
silence prompts the moment it lands, waits for the goodbye to finish
playing plus a grace the candidate's own goodbye extends, then ends
the session through the normal path: recording uploaded, scoring
started, report next. A paraphrased goodbye degrades to exactly the
old behavior. The closing sentence is now a cross-language contract,
pinned by twin gates in both trees.

**The measurement stack got a ruler you can version** (F-70, DECISIONS
051). Every Morgan naturalness metrics document records which
transcription model and DSP constants measured it; a committed
synthetic clip pins segmentation against config drift; and the
three-way `silence_duration_ms` duplicate the repo's own contract
called "equal by luck" is now equal by twin gates on both sides of the
language boundary.

## Fixed

**A long answer is no longer a dead connection** (DECISIONS 052). Live
sessions died "Connection lost" twenty seconds into the first
substantial answer: the connection guard read candidate-audible
silence as transport death, but server-side speech detection only
speaks at boundaries — a talking candidate produces no traffic by
design. Candidate speech now expects nothing; genuinely dead
transports are still caught four ways. Same incident: the microphone
permission survives client-side navigation into the room, and the
diagnostics disclosure opens scrolled to its newest entries.

**The session replay actually plays.** The recording strip had never
worked in production — the security policy's `media-src` omitted the
storage origin the signed URLs live on, so the browser refused the
fetch before a byte moved. Storage logs show sign requests succeeding
for days and zero reads, ever. The origin is now derived from the same
single source the connection allowlist uses, pinned against a second
hardcoded hostname.

## The gate

All four eval suites passed at their baselines on the release
candidate, exit 0 — pure regression, as intended for a release whose
risk lived outside the scoring stack. Numbers of record:
[evals/reports/2026-08-08-v012-gate.md](../../evals/reports/2026-08-08-v012-gate.md).

Migrations this release: 009 (feedback), 010 (session coaching),
011 (study materials, dormant). All three were applied to production
before the deploys that read them.

## Process, in the open

This release ran as eleven agent tracks across three batches, each
behind an adversarial review with fix authority and a verification
round. The reviews caught, before merge: a bookmark that vanished on
dialog reopen, an anti-enumeration test comparing a response to
itself, axis labels up to 7% off their data points, a pastel data line
measured below its own grid's contrast, a test seed defeated by
floating-point dust (twice — once from each side of the review), and
85% of an adversarial soup silently unchecked. Two earlier attempts by
coding agents to satisfy gates by obfuscating literals are on the
record in RETRO.md, and hunting that class is now a standing review
lens, also in the open in CLAUDE.md and PLAYBOOK.md.
