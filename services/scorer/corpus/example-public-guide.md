# General behavioral interview answer guide

A strong behavioral answer leads with the outcome, then the method. Name
the result first ("we cut churn by 12 percent"), then how you got there,
then your specific role in the work. Quantify wherever the truth allows.

Interviewers across industries probe for specifics: the metric that moved,
the trade-off you accepted, and what you would do differently. Vague
collective claims ("we improved the process") score poorly against
behaviorally anchored rubrics; one concrete, quantified example beats
three generic ones.

Pacing is part of the answer. Address the question asked within the first
two sentences, then expand. When you need a moment to think, say so
briefly instead of filling the silence.
